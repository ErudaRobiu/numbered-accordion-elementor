perspective

How close the eye is

Set on the parent, not the thing that turns. It is the distance from the viewer to the plane of the screen: a small number puts the eye right up against the element and the projection gets violent, a large one flattens towards an isometric view.

Under about 400px it stops reading as depth and starts reading as a fisheye. That threshold is the single most useful number on this page.

Three axes, three different feelings

X tips towards you and away, like a lid. Y turns like a door. Z is the only one that works without perspective at all, because it never leaves the plane of the screen — it is the flat spin you already have.

The colours here follow the engineering convention and stay consistent down the sheet, so a hue tells you which axis you are looking at.

translateZ() + transform-style

Depth between siblings

Pushing an element along Z only means something if its parent keeps a 3D space for it to sit in. That is transform-style: preserve-3d, and without it every child is projected onto the parent's own flat plane first — the depth is computed and then thrown away.

transform-origin

Where the hinge is

The same 52° turn three times over, with only the hinge moved. A rotation about the left edge reads as a door opening; about the centre it reads as an object being inspected. Nothing else changed.

This is the property that decides whether a motion feels like it belongs to the layout or like it was applied to it.

backface-visibility

What is on the other side

Turn a card past 90° and you are looking at the back of its front face — mirrored text, backwards. Hiding the backface and stacking a second face at rotateY(180deg) is what makes a card genuinely two-sided.

Switch it to visible and flip: the mirrored text is the bug this property exists to stop.

overflow · filter · opacity

The three properties that quietly kill it

Any of these on an ancestor forces it to flatten what is inside, whatever preserve-3d says. The depth is still being calculated; it is just being projected onto a single plane before you see it.

On a page builder this is nearly always the culprit: a section with overflow: hidden set to stop something else escaping.

What this would take as a module

Six properties, one shared perspective on a wrapper, and a pointer handler about fifteen lines long. The same shape as the motion module: presets for the common moves, the raw values exposed underneath for when a preset is nearly right.

The part worth building carefully is not the transform. It is the flattening — a widget dropped into a section with overflow: hidden has to degrade to its flat state rather than half-work, and it has to detect that rather than hope.
